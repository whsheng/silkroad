## Why the first 90 days should not be judged by volume alone

New Amazon sellers rarely fail because they cannot launch ads. They usually fail because margins, inventory, and operating rhythm are still unstable when they try to scale. The first 90 days should be used to prove that the product can sell repeatedly and profitably.

## Seven essentials to stabilize early operations

1. Build the real margin model across selling price, platform fees, FBA cost, freight, refund loss, ad spend ratio, and FX movement.
2. Keep the listing clear before trying to make it perfect so buyers understand the product fast.
3. Treat advertising as a learning system in the first 30 days and narrow decisions through search-term quality.
4. Run a conservative replenishment plan based on real weekly velocity and lead-time variance.
5. Separate account permissions and keep clean access patterns to reduce avoidable account risk.
6. Review conversion, ad, inventory, and refund data on a regular operating cadence.
7. Scale budget and SKU count only after the full operating loop starts repeating predictably.

## Risks new sellers often underestimate

Inventory discipline matters more than many new sellers expect. Stockouts interrupt both ranking and ad learning, while overstock strains cash flow. Revenue alone is a misleading signal if fulfilment and returns are eating the contribution margin.

> A repeatable operating loop is more valuable than a noisy early sales spike.
